## 2. Recommended four (in scroll order) and how the 3D reacts

H100 SXM facts the 3D can encode: 132 SMs (out of 144 on the die), 5 active HBM3 stacks on 6 sites, 3.35 TB/s, about 989 dense BF16 TFLOP/s.

**1. Roofline: "decode starves the SMs."** Draw the H100 roofs. The decode dot sits at about 1 FLOP/B, which is about 3.4 TFLOP/s or 0.3% of peak. Scrolling scrubs batch size from 1 to 256, and the dot climbs the slope to the ridge. A prefill dot then lands on the flat roof.
*3D:* the HBM stacks glow at full while the dot is on the slope. All 132 SMs take a brightness equal to achieved ÷ peak FLOP/s: 0.3% at batch 1, about 22% at batch 64, full at the ridge. Don't switch SMs off: memory-bound kernels still occupy every SM while waiting on HBM, and engineers will notice. Packets stream from the stacks across the interposer. Prefill reverses the picture: the SM array floods and the HBM dims.

**2. HBM budget: sizing → paging → caching** (Kevin's GPU-sizing and caching work). A stacked bar against 80 GB. Llama 3.1 8B in BF16 is about 16 GB of weights. KV cache = 2 × 32 layers × 8 KV heads × 128 × 2 B = 128 KiB per token, so about 400K tokens fit, or about 50 concurrent 8K-token chats after overhead. Then toggle contiguous allocation to paged (20.4% → 96.3% useful). Then a prefix-hit slider makes the TTFT and $ bars fall.
*3D:* the stacks fill like gauges. Weights form a solid base; KV fills in as page tiles per conversation. Fragmentation shows as red gaps that close when paging switches on. Cache hits make shared tiles glow and fan out to several streams while the SMs skip that prefill. Leave the sixth HBM site dark.

**3. Pareto frontier → $/1M tokens** (the serving trade-off, then tokenomics). Use the InferenceMAX axes with concurrency dots and the hull. An optimised curve rises (spec decoding/MTP, FP4, disaggregation). Then the y-axis morphs into $/1M tokens and the curve flips. Pin 100 tok/s/user, where NVIDIA reports $0.11 → $0.02.
*3D:* a concurrency playhead drives the die. At the throughput end, hundreds of thin token streams leave the die (reuse the tokenizer chips from the inference section) and the HBM pulses slowly. One pulse is one decode step, which reads the full weights plus KV. Moving toward interactivity, the streams thin out and the pulse speeds up, since per-user tok/s ≈ decode steps per second. With speculation, each pulse emits 2–3 chips per stream. During the $ morph, a fixed "$/GPU-hour" plate sits on the board and the token counter drives the price.

**4. Optimisation staircase** (a closer for recruiters; use Kevin's own measured numbers).
*3D:* each step toggles one mechanism. CUDA graphs fill the idle gaps in an SM activity strip. INT8/INT4 shrink the HBM packets to ½ and ¼ size, so the pulses speed up. Speculation shows ghost tokens being confirmed. An MBU readout climbs.

I'd leave two out of this section. The provider price/speed scatter is about choosing a vendor, which is not Kevin's work. Raw CDFs are too dense for recruiters, so show p50/p99 TTFT as a small readout on chart 3 instead.
