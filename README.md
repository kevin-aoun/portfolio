# Kevin Aoun, portfolio

A full-screen scroll story drawn as line art, told as Kevin's loop (listen, build, keep it loved, run it lean), then the work:

1. **The opening.** One transistor, with electrons flashing through it in irregular bursts, and a small "Scroll" at the bottom that breathes until the visitor scrolls.
2. **Hey, I'm Kevin.** The name in its real tokens (o200k_base, each ID counting up under its token), Kevin's line (he works where sales, engineering and customer success meet), and "Book a call", with the transistor larger in the top right.
3. **Four make a decision** (listen). The camera pulls out to a NAND gate: four transistors make a decision, and Kevin's work starts with one, in discovery with clients alongside sales. The camera keeps pulling out while the chip builds itself without words: a streaming multiprocessor, the H100 die, its HBM package closing up, the SXM board, where **Then build it, layer by layer** (build) says what he took from prototype to production at Gaya and BMW.
4. **Now it can talk.** One large line crosses the screen, lighting up word by word, while the chip powers on: the voice AI and RAG he shipped at inmind.
5. **A body.** The chip goes into a robot: Unitree's G1 humanoid, from its published CAD meshes (every link with its motors, brackets and bolts). Its parts come on around the chip as the camera pulls back and the chip rises out of the way. The arms assemble link by link from shoulder to fingertips, the camera pulls out to the torso, then the legs. The chip comes back down into place, the head closes over it like a helmet, the die flashes, and as the camera pulls out to the whole robot a light-up runs head to toe through the five token colours. "Give it a body" (keep it loved): shipped work lives with people, so his evals, his team's dashboard and his teaching keep it working for them.
6. **What fits in 80 GB** (run it lean). The head opens and the camera dives into the chip: Kevin sizes GPUs and cut clients' hosting by 37%, and the chip's memory is his own: 16 GB of weights, loaded at birth, then a KV cache his skills fill tile by tile in his shares of it (Python & FastAPI 20%, tokenomics, GPU sizing & optimization and AI fundamentals 10% each), with the rest, 50%, in reserve for hiking and MMA. The shares and colours are `SKILLS` in `src/graphs/data.js`; the reserve is what they leave (`RESERVE`). This panel stays up as the story scrolls away.
7. **The rest.** Certifications (a centred row of badges for Claude, NVIDIA and Microsoft), projects (led by what Kevin does off the clock; each with its own drawing; the live avatar's is a live orb, as on the avatar's own stage, acting out a turn: listening, a tool call, speaking; on phones and tablets the projects are a swiped carousel of cards, a square drawing over the words, with dots under it), writing, the work timeline (on desktop a profiler-style trace over the list of roles; on phones and tablets one vertical timeline whose rail fills as you read and whose role at mid-screen lights up), and "Write to me", where a message shows the way a model reads it. Book a call is the main call to action, tinted in Kevin's red with a light running round its edge (centred on phones); the links out under it (LinkedIn, GitHub, Email, Blog, X) copy the blog's: an arrow, then the name. Each section's heading is a soft snap point, so a scroll that ends near one settles on it. The footer is a grove of Kevin's tree mark: small, upright and quiet, with one mark in Kevin's token colour (red) as a signature. The trees follow the pointer: the ones near it lean away, grow a little and glow faintly, and now and then one lights up in a token colour; then they settle back, eased, never sprung. It hides a puzzle: click lit colours in the right order (`ORDER` in `src/footer/field.js`) and each stays on; a wrong one clears them; all five turn the whole grove gold.

Anywhere on the 3D stage, dragging sideways turns the subject about the vertical; three seconds after you let go it eases back. On touch screens a sideways swipe turns it and an up-and-down one still scrolls.

**Colours.** The page is blocks, the way a long website is: a block in one of Kevin's own two-colour templates (`templates/`, kept out of the repo; sampled from the images, `PALETTES` in `src/palettes.js`), then a plain block, then another palette. Each palette is a pair of colours with a side for each theme: on the dark theme a dark ground with a light ink (burgundy with pink, rust with yellow, navy with azure), on paper the inverse (pink with burgundy, and so on). Where Kevin made both sides as templates (rust and amber, forest and sage, aubergine and pink), each theme takes its own; the others swap. Teal and indigo are two more pairs in the same manner, so the seven palette blocks never repeat one. They are dealt anew on every visit. Each story beat with words holds a palette while its words are up; between them, and through the wordless chip build and robot assembly, the stage is the plain block. In a palette block everything is the ground, the ink or a tint of the ink: the 3D drawing is remapped in the edge shader (brightness sets each pixel's place between the pair's dark and light colour), the text, tokens and charts by CSS variables (`[data-pal]` in `base.css`, with each side's muted text set as light as it can go and still pass AA). Below the story the sections take turns: Certifications plain, Projects a palette, Writing plain, Work a palette, then "Write to me" and the footer as one plain block (the live tokenizer and the tree puzzle need the five token colours). Every block carries the templates' paper grain.

The plain block is the theme itself. Dark is the default: warm black. Its colour comes from the tokenizer's five token colours (purple, green, amber, red, blue): OpenAI's own on the dark page, pushed to fuller chroma at the same lightness, and deepened in OKLCH on paper so each passes AA as text in both themes (`--c0` to `--c4` in `base.css`, with the tokens' tints as `--tk0` to `--tk4`). Lists take them in order through `--hue`, the way a tokenizer colours consecutive tokens: each organisation on the work trace, each project, post and contact link, the memory chart's skills, the 3D callouts' dots and the electrons. Light is paper with the same colours. Behind the story, on the 3D stage's empty ground, is a brush: a faint field of dots, one per character cell (10 × 18 px, from the screen's top left), that the pointer or a finger paints. A stroke is laid down along the path, broad and full when slow, thin and lighter when fast, its edges frayed by a fixed grain per cell; its characters ink up the ramp `.` `:` then the stroke's own hatching (`-` `\` `|` `/`, by its direction) `+` `#`, now and then one in a token colour, and fade back down it within a second or so. `src/brush.js` keeps the map of ink and direction (`FADE`, the cell size `CW` × `CH`); the stage's edge shader draws a glyph per cell from it, out of an atlas of the page's monospace (`GLYPHS` and the resting and inked strength `grid` in `PALETTES`, in `src/scene/scene.js`). The bar leads with Kevin's tree mark in ink; on phones the mark alone stands for the name. Buttons wear the bar's glass, except the call to action. Switch themes with the button in the bar or `?theme=light`.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173/
npm run build      # production build in dist/
npm run preview    # serve dist/
```

It deploys as static files (the contact box opens the visitor's mail app; nothing runs on a server): Cloudflare Workers builds it from GitHub on every push to `main` (`npm run build`, then `npx wrangler deploy`, which serves `dist/` as static assets per `wrangler.jsonc`). `public/_headers` lets browsers keep the hashed files under `/assets` for good. It is meant for https://kevinaoun.dev (the canonical and link-preview tags in `index.html`; the preview image is `public/og.jpg`), with the blog at https://blog.kevinaoun.dev.

## Notes

- **Hardware numbers.** They are the NVIDIA H100 SXM's: 80 billion transistors, 814 mm², 132 SMs, 528 tensor cores, 50 MB L2, five live HBM3 stacks, 80 GB at 3.35 TB/s, up to 700 W.
- **Scale.** Below the die the drawing is schematic. The scale bar shows the real width at each step; the fin pitch is about 28 nm.
- **Electrons.** They cross only while the gate is on, in bursts. That is how a real switch behaves: current flows while the output node discharges, then stops.
- **The memory chart's pace.** What the GPU does while the cache fills is modelled, not measured: Llama 3.1 8B on one H100 SXM (`.taste-skill/research/`).
- **Contact price.** The contact box's reading cost is illustrative ($3 per million input tokens) and says so.
- **Token IDs.** They are real o200k_base IDs (GPT-4o's tokenizer), and the page says so under every Token IDs view; Claude's tokenizer is not public. The token colours are the five OpenAI uses.
- **`?shot`.** Turns off scroll smoothing, for screenshots.
- **Performance.** Every shader compiles at load, so nothing stalls mid-scroll. The screen gets no depth buffer (nothing drawn there is depth-tested). On a machine without GPU acceleration the stage steps its resolution down to 60%.
- **No WebGL.** The panels still tell the story over an empty stage, without the scale bar.
- **Reduced motion.** The camera moves in steps, panels fade instead of moving, and there are no electrons or transitions. The robot's parts jump into place and it doesn't light up; a turned subject snaps back.
- **The orb.** The live avatar's card is drawn with `thinking-orbs` (MIT, Jakub Antalik), the library the avatar's own client uses, through its framework-free engine (`thinking-orbs/engine`), so no React reaches the page. It draws only while on screen, and holds still under reduced motion.
- **The robot.** The G1 meshes are Unitree's, BSD-3-Clause, simplified to about 54k triangles (0.5 MB, loaded once the page is idle); the notice ships as `public/robot-LICENSE.txt`. The Unitree logo plate is left out, and nothing claims Unitree's endorsement.
