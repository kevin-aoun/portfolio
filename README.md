# Kevin Aoun, portfolio

A full-screen scroll story drawn as line art, then the work:

1. **The opening.** One transistor, with electrons flashing through it in irregular bursts.
2. **Hey, I'm Kevin.** The name in its real tokens (o200k_base, each ID counting up under its token), a line on what Kevin does, "Book a call" and his email, with the transistor larger in the top right.
3. **Four make a decision.** The camera pulls out to a NAND gate, then keeps pulling out while the chip builds itself without words: a streaming multiprocessor, the H100 die, its HBM package closing up, the SXM board.
4. **Now it can talk.** One large line crosses the screen, lighting up word by word, while the chip powers on.
5. **A body.** The chip goes into a robot: Unitree's G1 humanoid, from its published CAD meshes (every link with its motors, brackets and bolts). Its parts come on around the chip as the camera pulls back and the chip rises out of the way. The arms assemble link by link from shoulder to fingertips, the camera pulls out to the torso, then the legs. The chip comes back down into place, the head closes over it like a helmet, the die flashes, and as the camera pulls out to the whole robot a light-up runs head to toe through the five token colours. "Give it a body": Kevin is a mechatronics engineer, on the technical and the client side.
6. **What fits in 80 GB.** The head opens and the camera dives into the chip, whose memory is Kevin's: 16 GB of weights, preloaded at birth, then a KV cache his skills fill tile by tile in his shares of it (Python 20%, FastAPI 10%, tokenomics 10%, cost optimization 20%, GPU sizing 10%, AI fundamentals 10%, MMA 5%, hiking 5%), with 10% in reserve. The shares and colours are `SKILLS` in `src/graphs/data.js`. This panel stays up as the story scrolls away.
7. **The rest.** Certifications (a centred row of badges for Claude, NVIDIA and Microsoft), projects (each with its own drawing; the live avatar's is a live orb, as on the avatar's own stage, acting out a turn: listening, a tool call, speaking), writing, the work timeline, and "Write to me", where a message shows the way a model reads it. The footer is a grove of Kevin's tree mark: small, upright and quiet, with one mark in Kevin's token colour (red) as a signature. The trees follow the pointer: the ones near it lean away, grow a little and glow faintly, and now and then one lights up in a token colour; then they settle back, eased, never sprung. It hides a puzzle: click lit colours in the right order (`ORDER` in `src/footer/field.js`) and each stays on; a wrong one clears them; all five turn the whole grove gold.

Anywhere on the 3D stage, dragging sideways turns the subject about the vertical; three seconds after you let go it eases back. On touch screens a sideways swipe turns it and an up-and-down one still scrolls.

Dark is the default theme: warm black. Colour comes from the tokenizer's five token colours (purple, green, amber, red, blue): OpenAI's own on the dark page, pushed to fuller chroma at the same lightness, and deepened in OKLCH on paper so each passes AA as text in both themes (`--c0` to `--c4` in `base.css`, with the tokens' tints as `--tk0` to `--tk4`). Lists take them in order through `--hue`, the way a tokenizer colours consecutive tokens: each organisation on the work trace, each project, post, skill and contact link, the memory chart's skills, the 3D callouts' dots and the electrons. Light is paper with the same colours. Behind everything sits a drafting grid of 80 px squares (a stronger line every fourth), fixed to the screen and dim at rest; wherever the pointer or a finger brushes, the nearby lines brighten, then fade back. `src/grid.js` draws it behind the page and keeps the brush map, and the 3D stage draws the same lines from that map on its empty ground, so the two line up (`--grid-*` in `base.css`, `grid` in the stage's `PALETTES`, the resting share `DIM` in `grid.js`). Under the grid is an aura: firm-edged pools of warm light, red and pink with yellow burning inside them, with a fine grain over them and none over the content. On paper they are tinted washes. During the story the light sits around the subject and each beat arranges it its own way: red under the transistor, pink over it and yellow between, all brightening while current flows; then the fire moves at each scale and stays low under the memory chart; the name has its own, red for Kevin's token. The robot's light-up runs it through five colours. It only ever sits behind: the 3D parts fade into the unlit ground, never into the light, text printed on them stays over it, and its brightness rolls off under a ceiling (`cap` in `AURA`), so it never outshines the outlines or the text. Below the story, Certifications, Projects, Writing and Work each have their own, mixed by scroll; the contact band and the footer are surfaces over it, so the light stops at the band's top edge, and the timeline's year bands are solid. `src/aura.js` holds the moods (`STAGE`, `PAGE`) and draws them behind the page; the stage draws the same lights, grain included, on its ground, so there is no seam where it scrolls away. The bar leads with Kevin's tree mark in ink; on phones the mark alone stands for the name. Buttons wear the bar's glass. Switch with the button in the bar or `?theme=light`.

**Beta colours.** The bar's "Beta" button (or `?look=beta`) turns the page into blocks in Kevin's own two-colour templates (`templates/`, kept out of the repo), sampled from the images (`PALETTES` in `src/beta.js`). Each palette is a pair of colours with a side for each theme: on the dark theme a dark ground with a light ink (burgundy with pink, rust with yellow, navy with azure), on paper the inverse (pink with burgundy, and so on). Where Kevin made both sides as templates (rust and amber, forest and sage, aubergine and pink), each theme takes its own; the others swap. Teal and indigo are two more pairs in the same manner, so the seven palette blocks never repeat one. They are dealt anew on every visit. Each story beat with words holds a palette while its words are up; between them, and through the wordless chip build and robot assembly, the stage is the plain block, which is the theme itself (black or paper) with its token colours. In a palette block everything is the ground, the ink or a tint of the ink: the 3D drawing is remapped in the edge shader (brightness sets each pixel's place between the pair's dark and light colour), the text, tokens and charts by CSS variables (`[data-pal]` in `base.css`, with each side's muted text set as light as it can go and still pass AA). Below the story the sections take turns: Certifications plain, Projects a palette, Writing plain, Work a palette, then "Write to me" and the footer as one plain block (the live tokenizer and the tree puzzle need the five token colours). The aura is off in beta; each block carries the template's paper grain. The choice is remembered, and the theme button works in beta too.

## Run it

```bash
npm install
npm run dev        # http://localhost:5173/
npm run build      # production build in dist/
npm run preview    # serve dist/
```

It deploys as static files (the contact box opens the visitor's mail app; nothing runs on a server): Cloudflare Workers builds it from GitHub on every push to `main` (`npm run build`, then `npx wrangler deploy`, which serves `dist/` as static assets per `wrangler.jsonc`). `public/_headers` lets browsers keep the hashed files under `/assets` for good. It is meant for https://kevinaoun.dev (the canonical and link-preview tags in `index.html`; the preview image is `public/og.jpg`), with the blog at https://blog.kevinaoun.dev.

## Notes

- **Hardware numbers.** They are the NVIDIA H100 SXM's: 80 billion transistors, 814 mm², 132 SMs, 528 tensor cores, 50 MB L2, five live HBM3 stacks, 80 GB at 3.35 TB/s, up to 700 W.
- **Scale.** Below the die the drawing is schematic. The scale bar shows the real width at each step; the fin pitch is about 28 nm.
- **Electrons.** They cross only while the gate is on, in bursts. That is how a real switch behaves: current flows while the output node discharges, then stops.
- **The memory chart's pace.** What the GPU does while the cache fills is modelled, not measured: Llama 3.1 8B on one H100 SXM (`.taste-skill/research/`).
- **Contact price.** The contact box's reading cost is illustrative ($3 per million input tokens) and says so.
- **Token IDs.** They are real o200k_base IDs (GPT-4o's tokenizer), and the page says so under every Token IDs view; Claude's tokenizer is not public. The token colours are the five OpenAI uses.
- **`?shot`.** Turns off scroll smoothing, for screenshots.
- **Performance.** Every shader compiles at load, so nothing stalls mid-scroll. The page's aura canvas only draws the rows the opaque 3D stage and the contact band leave visible. The screen gets no depth buffer (nothing drawn there is depth-tested). On a machine without GPU acceleration the stage steps its resolution down to 60%.
- **No WebGL.** The panels still tell the story over an empty stage, without the scale bar.
- **Reduced motion.** The camera moves in steps, panels fade instead of moving, and there are no electrons or transitions. The robot's parts jump into place and it doesn't light up; a turned subject snaps back.
- **The orb.** The live avatar's card is drawn with `thinking-orbs` (MIT, Jakub Antalik), the library the avatar's own client uses, through its framework-free engine (`thinking-orbs/engine`), so no React reaches the page. It draws only while on screen, and holds still under reduced motion.
- **The robot.** The G1 meshes are Unitree's, BSD-3-Clause, simplified to about 54k triangles (0.5 MB, loaded once the page is idle); the notice ships as `public/robot-LICENSE.txt`. The Unitree logo plate is left out, and nothing claims Unitree's endorsement.
